## Seven-step SPS founder diagnosis

Case: Northline Heating (fictional). Monthly revenue $180,000; direct costs $108,000; overhead $54,000 including $12,000 acquisition spend. 60 new customers.

Questions asked: monthly capacity/bookings/backlog/callbacks; P&L and acquisition definitions; cash and customer complaints. Five evidence categories were released before submission.

Define the Problem: Define the problem as a growing installation backlog and longer waits despite rising sales. Confirm a two-week diagnostic window and a 90-day improvement goal with the founder. The growth figures are facts; the marginal economics of another crew remain unknown.

Break Down the Problem: Break operating performance into demand, conversion, delivery capacity, quality and cash. Compare a dispatch/capacity hypothesis with a rework/quality hypothesis. Separately test whether bad-fit sales create longer jobs rather than assuming every booking is equal.

Plan the Work: Plan the work: request crew hours, travel time, job duration, backlog age and callbacks by installation. The operations lead should produce a five-day baseline. Finance should provide a 13-week cash forecast before any hiring commitment.

Work the Plan: Work the plan: 75 booked jobs minus 60 completed means backlog grows by 15 jobs per month. Stable 3% callbacks and stable satisfaction weaken the quality hypothesis. Profit is $180,000 minus $108,000 minus $54,000 = $18,000; operating margin is 10%. CAC is $12,000 divided by 60 = $200. Delivery capacity and dispatch are the likely primary constraint, not lack of demand.

Build the Case: Build the case: compare better dispatch, extra crew capacity, and more advertising. Pilot geographic scheduling first because nearby jobs are currently placed on different days. Assume $1,200 gross contribution per additional delivered job only if the current $3,000 price and $1,800 direct cost are marginally representative; confirm that before estimating up to $18,000 from 15 additional jobs. Cash timing is still unknown.

Make the Case: Make the case: I recommend a two-week dispatch pilot before increasing ads. Current demand exceeds delivery by 15 jobs monthly, while measured quality is stable. We should test whether routing frees usable capacity and protects contribution. I invite a peer to challenge the diagnosis; I would correct it if crew-time or customer-fit evidence explains the gap better.

Start the Change: Run a two-week dispatch pilot. Track baseline backlog age, completed jobs and callback rate. Target no further backlog growth while keeping callbacks at or below 3%. Review each Friday and stop or revise if quality falls or throughput does not improve.
